  <section>
    <h2>About Gourav</h2>
    <p>Gourav Mishra is a Business Analyst with a practical focus on how people actually work with documents and data. His perspective is less about describing a feature from a specification and more about checking whether a workflow makes sense when a person has a real file, a deadline, and a specific outcome to achieve.</p>
    <p>At DataFrog, Gourav contributes to practical guides and tool content where the important questions are often simple: What happens after I click the button? Does the output remain usable? What changes when the input is messy? Which result is good enough for sharing, editing, printing, or analysis?</p>
  </section>

  <section>
    <h2>What he focuses on</h2>
    <ul>
      <li>Real-world document and data workflows</li>
      <li>Comparing expected results with actual output</li>
      <li>Usability issues that are easy to miss in a technical specification</li>
      <li>Practical explanations for non-specialist users</li>
      <li>Testing workflows with different kinds of input rather than relying on one ideal example</li>
    </ul>
  </section>

  <section>
    <h2>His approach to DataFrog content</h2>
    <p>Gourav's articles are written from the user's side of the desk. A conversion is not considered successful merely because a file was produced. The resulting file has to open, retain the important information, and behave reasonably for the task it was created for.</p>
    <p>That is particularly important for document tools. A smaller PDF that becomes difficult to read is not automatically a better result. A converted Word document that opens but needs extensive manual repair is not equivalent to a clean conversion. His contribution is to document those practical differences clearly.</p>
  </section>

When an article carries his name, its practical observations are based on checking the workflow and output rather than simply repeating a feature description.</p>
